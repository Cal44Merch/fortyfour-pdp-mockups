# Scoring rubric (all disciplines, 0–10)

Score against what a competent professional in your discipline would expect of a
serious commercial website — not against perfection, and not against the average
of the web (which is bad).

- **9–10** Exemplary. You'd show this as a positive case study. Zero critical or
  high issues; minor polish at most.
- **7–8** Solid. Fundamentals right, no critical issues, a handful of
  medium/high issues with clear fixes.
- **5–6** Mixed. At least one critical-adjacent problem or a pattern of
  highs; measurably losing users/revenue/rankings, but recoverable with
  focused work.
- **3–4** Poor. Critical issues present; the discipline is actively hurting the
  business. Needs prioritised remediation, not polish.
- **0–2** Failing. Fundamentally broken or absent (e.g. site unusable on
  mobile, no HTTPS, unreadable content).

Severity definitions (used in findings):
- **critical** — losing users/money/data right now, or a legal/safety exposure
- **high** — materially hurts conversion, ranking, trust, or maintainability
- **medium** — clear improvement with measurable benefit
- **low** — polish; worth doing when nearby code is touched

Effort: **S** ≤ a day · **M** ≤ a week · **L** > a week / needs a project.

Calibration rules:
- A 10 must be defensible to a sceptical expert; an unjustified 10 is worse
  than an honest 7.
- The score must be consistent with the issues list: critical issue present →
  score ≤ 5; nothing above medium → score ≥ 7.
- Missing evidence lowers confidence, not the score — say what you couldn't
  assess.
